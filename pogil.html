Process Oriented Guided Inquiry Learning

Support the inquiry.
Make the thinking visible.

coLearn-AI brings guided questions, shared group work, and instructor insight together to support POGIL activities.

POGIL combines small-team learning with carefully designed activities that help students develop concepts and practice process skills. Students build understanding through inquiry, while the instructor facilitates their learning. Learn about the POGIL approach.

From pedagogy to practice

A workspace for purposeful group learning.

01 / GUIDED INQUIRY

Structure the learning cycle

Design a sequence in which students explore a model or example, develop an explanation, and apply their understanding to a new situation. coLearn’s activity builder and text or code components give instructors a place to organize that sequence.

02 / TEAM ROLES

Give participation a purpose

Use coLearn’s facilitator, analyst, quality-check, and spokesperson roles to distribute responsibilities. Shared work and turn rotation help make contribution part of the activity, with role expectations explained by the instructor.

03 / PROCESS SKILLS

Practice how to learn together

Ask students to explain evidence, compare interpretations, reach agreement, and reflect on their teamwork. Shared responses and revisions give groups concrete work to discuss as they practice communication, critical thinking, and problem solving.

04 / FACILITATION

Coach while the work unfolds

Course, activity, and group views help instructors follow progress and identify where a question or conversation may help. The record of responses, revisions, and participation supports follow-up discussion and reflection.

05 / AI SCAFFOLDING

Keep reasoning with the students

Use AI prompts and feedback to encourage explanation and examination of evidence. For a POGIL activity, instructors should review that guidance so it supports discovery and leaves the group responsible for constructing its answer.

06 / ACTIVITY DESIGN

Keep the instructor in control

Create or refine an activity, preview its questions, and revise before launching it for a course. Effective POGIL still depends on thoughtful models, question sequences, explicit process-skill goals, and classroom facilitation.

An example activity

Explore a network. Build an explanation.

This sample sequence shows how an instructor could organize a guided-inquiry activity in coLearn-AI.

  1. Explore

    Give the group a small network diagram. Ask students to trace possible routes and describe what happens when a connection is removed.

  2. Develop a concept

    Have students compare observations and explain which features keep the network connected. Ask the group to support its explanation with evidence from the model.

  3. Apply and reflect

    Present a different network and ask students to predict its behavior. Finish with a prompt about how their roles and discussion improved the group’s reasoning.

The instructor can follow group progress and use students’ explanations to choose when to ask a probing question or bring the class together.

Plan your next guided-inquiry activity.

Start with a learning goal, a model worth investigating, and a process skill for students to practice. Then use coLearn-AI to organize the questions and make the group’s reasoning available for feedback.
